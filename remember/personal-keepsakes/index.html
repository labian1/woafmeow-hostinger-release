Personal Keepsakes

Small, personal pieces shaped around a paw print, nose print, photograph, name, or story.

Choose the meaning before the object.

For families who want a small object to hold, wear, or give. A thoughtful keepsake starts with a clear consent and personalization process, accurate proofing, and a maker who explains the material, timeline, and what they need from you.

Read the aftercare guide

Three checks that protect the memory.

1

Capture prints before the hard day

If a paw or nose print matters to you, ask your veterinary or aftercare team early whether they can help. Ink, clay, and digital impressions all have different limits.

2

Keep the original image file

Save the highest-resolution photo and a close-up of any print in two places. It gives you more freedom if you decide to make something later.

3

Check the proof before it is made

For engraving or custom art, review the spelling, print orientation, crop, material, dimensions, and delivery estimate. A careful maker makes this step easy.

A few useful next steps.

Keep the care details clear If aftercare is part of the decision, write down the provider, identity process, transport plan, return estimate, and the one thing you do not want missed. Find source-labeled support Use the directory when you need a local aftercare, cemetery, cremation, aquamation, grief, or legacy-making service to start the conversation. Give a family member a role One person can hold the questions, one can keep the photos, and one can make the calls. Sharing the practical work can make a hard day gentler.
