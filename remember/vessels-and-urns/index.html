Vessels & Urns

A quiet place to hold remains, chosen in your own time and for your own home.

Choose the meaning before the object.

For families considering a vessel after cremation or aquamation. There is no required timeline and no single right format. The future collection should make materials, capacity, closure, personalization, and return expectations easy to understand before an order is placed.

Read the aftercare guide

Three checks that protect the memory.

1

Ask what will be returned

Confirm individual, partitioned, or communal care; the provider's identity process; what material is returned; the expected volume; and when it will be ready. Terms vary by provider and place.

2

Choose for the space you have

Some families want a visible vessel. Others prefer a keepsake, burial, scattering, or a private place. You do not need to decide what is right for anyone else.

3

Check capacity and closure

A supplier should state capacity, dimensions, closure type, material, personalization limits, and care instructions plainly. Ask before buying if any detail is missing.

A few useful next steps.

Keep the care details clear If aftercare is part of the decision, write down the provider, identity process, transport plan, return estimate, and the one thing you do not want missed. Find source-labeled support Use the directory when you need a local aftercare, cemetery, cremation, aquamation, grief, or legacy-making service to start the conversation. Give a family member a role One person can hold the questions, one can keep the photos, and one can make the calls. Sharing the practical work can make a hard day gentler.
