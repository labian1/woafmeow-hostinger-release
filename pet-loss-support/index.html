Make a few decisions before they have to be made.

For the dog or cat you love: keep comfort, care contacts, and aftercare wishes in one calm place.

Start a quality-of-life noteFind comfort care

Ask about the moment you are in

Describe what changed, what your veterinarian has said, and what feels hardest today.

Keep these answers together.

What still feels like a good day? Name two routines your pet still enjoys, the hard moment you do not want to ignore, and what comfort means in your home. Who should be called first? Save your regular veterinarian, emergency hospital, and any hospice or in-home team before a late-night decision has to be made. What would you want aftercare to include? Ask about timing, transport, identity handling, return options, and which keepsake requests need to be made before the appointment.

Separate the care decision from the memory decision.

Start with the care your pet needs today.

Hospice, palliative care, euthanasia, and aftercare decisions can be emotional and time-sensitive. A frame, keepsake, tree, or urn can wait until you have the information and room to choose without pressure.

Three questions that make the next conversation clearer.

1

What changes should prompt a call?

Ask your veterinarian what is urgent, what can wait for a scheduled visit, and who should be called after hours.

2

What should comfort look like this week?

Ask about pain, appetite, sleep, toileting, mobility, anxiety, and the specific signs that the plan is helping.

3

What will happen after the appointment?

Confirm transport, aftercare options, identity handling, return timing, and whether your family wants a print, clipping, or private time.
