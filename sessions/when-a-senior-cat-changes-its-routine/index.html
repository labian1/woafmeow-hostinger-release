When a senior cat changes its routine

A careful way to notice lower jumps, quieter meals, grooming changes, hiding, and litter-box shifts without asking your cat to perform.

Reserve my placeAll care sessions

What you can use right away.

Watch the routine your cat chose Use a familiar jump, sleep spot, meal, or litter-box visit instead of a stressful test. Record the pattern safely A ten-second ordinary video and a few dates can be more useful than trying to recreate the behavior. Know the sooner-call questions Build a concise note for appetite, pain, mobility, grooming, or bathroom changes.

The hour, in real terms.

01

The quieter change How to notice a lower jump, a new hiding place, or a different way of eating without assuming the reason.

02

The litter-box story What timing, output, hesitation, vocalizing, or repeated trips can help a veterinary team understand.

03

The next practical step Choose one change to observe and one question to bring forward.

One clear observation can change the next conversation.

Bring the detail you would otherwise lose: a time, a familiar routine, a short ordinary video, the recovery afterward, or what helped. The goal is not to self-diagnose. It is to make the next question more useful.
