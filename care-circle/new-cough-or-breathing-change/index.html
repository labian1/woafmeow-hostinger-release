A new cough or breathing change

Separate an occasional sound from a repeated breathing pattern and know which signs should not wait.

Dog
Cooper · 8 years · Cocker Spaniel
Owner-shared conditions
heart murmur
What changed
a new cough while resting after dinner
Why this matters for Cooper

Cooper is 8 years, a Cocker Spaniel, with heart murmur; the owner reports a new cough while resting after dinner.

Your public Care Circle post

You can remove this post and its public lesson whenever you choose.

Record one natural breathing moment

You will capture timing, sound, posture, and recovery without provoking another episode.

Observe your dog at rest. Note what happened immediately before the change, how long it lasted, and how your dog recovered.

  1. Record whether your dog was asleep, resting, eating, drinking, or active.
  2. Note cough sound, breathing effort, posture, gum color, and time to settle.
  3. Never exercise or excite your dog to recreate a breathing change.
Which record is most useful?

Count resting breaths safely

You will have one calm resting count to share with the care team.

When your dog is fully asleep or quietly resting, count chest rises without touching or waking them.

  1. Count one rise and fall as one breath for 30 seconds, then double it.
  2. Repeat only at another naturally calm time if your veterinarian asks.
  3. Stop counting and seek help if breathing looks difficult, gums change color, or your dog cannot settle.
When should you count?

Make the veterinary call specific

You will know what to report first and what needs emergency care.

Lead with onset, frequency, effort, gum color, recovery, and any paired appetite, energy, heart, or medicine change.

  1. Bring one short natural video if it can be taken safely.
  2. List heart, airway, allergy, pain, and current medicine history.
  3. Trouble breathing, blue or gray gums, collapse, or severe distress needs immediate care.
Which sign needs emergency help?

Send a breathing-event summary

Show when the cough or breathing change happens and what your dog was doing before it began.

Breathing changes deserve precise timing and a low threshold for urgent veterinary care.

  1. When your dog is fully asleep, count chest rises for 30 seconds and double the number; stop if this causes disturbance.
  2. Record posture, effort, sound, duration, activity, temperature and whether gums look normally colored.
  3. Share a safe short video, the resting count, medicines and the exact first date with the veterinary team.

Call sooner when you see this

Labored breathing, blue or gray gums, collapse, inability to settle, severe weakness, or choking needs immediate veterinary care.
