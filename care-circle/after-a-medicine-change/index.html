TRIAGE FIRST

Check urgency before tracking.

Before tracking, check whether the change is sudden, severe or worsening. Trouble breathing, facial swelling, collapse, seizure, repeated vomiting, profound weakness, suspected overdose, or a severe sudden reaction needs immediate veterinary care. Recording can support a veterinary conversation, but it must not delay care.

Build the dose-and-change timeline

You will show exactly when the new pattern began relative to each dose.

Record medicine name, amount, scheduled time, actual time, food, and the change that followed.

  1. Copy the label instructions exactly into the record.
  2. Note missed, late, vomited, or uncertain doses without correcting them yourself.
  3. Add eating, drinking, urine, stool, sleep, movement, and behavior changes.
What makes the timeline useful?

Protect the prescribed plan

You will know what to do while waiting for the prescribing team.

Do not stop, double, split, or substitute a prescription unless the veterinary team instructs you to do so.

  1. Call the prescribing clinic or its after-hours route with the timeline.
  2. Keep packaging, concentration, and all other medicines available for the call.
  3. Prevent access to dropped pills and never add human medicine.
What is the safest next step?

Recognize a possible severe reaction

You will know which signs need emergency help rather than a routine callback.

Severe breathing, swelling, collapse, seizures, repeated vomiting, profound weakness, or suspected overdose should not wait.

  1. Bring or photograph the label and note the possible amount involved.
  2. Call emergency veterinary care or poison guidance as directed locally.
  3. Do not induce vomiting unless a professional specifically instructs you.
Which situation needs urgent help?

Create a medicine-response timeline

Connect the drug, dose and timing with the exact new change.

A clear before-and-after timeline helps the prescribing team decide what deserves prompt review.

  1. Copy the medicine name, strength, dose, schedule, start date and prescribing clinic from the label.
  2. Record each new sign with time after the dose, food, water, bathroom and alertness changes.
  3. Contact the prescribing team with the timeline before stopping or changing a prescribed medicine unless emergency instructions say otherwise.
What should you send after a new medicine change?

Call sooner when you see this
