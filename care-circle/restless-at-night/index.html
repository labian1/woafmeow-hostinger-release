Why this matters for Luna

Luna is 10 years, a Labrador mix, with osteoarthritis; the owner reports wakes after midnight, paces and asks to go outside.

Build a 7-night wake-up timeline

You will see whether the same trigger, time, and behavior repeat.

Write the time, first behavior, where your dog went, what happened before bed, and what finally helped them settle.

  1. Separate pacing, panting, vocalizing, thirst, and toileting.
  2. Note meals, medicines, visitors, activity, and room temperature.
  3. Record ordinary nights; do not keep your dog awake to test a theory.

Reduce nighttime friction

You will know how to make tonight calmer without masking the pattern.

Keep the route predictable and change only one environmental detail at a time.

  1. Keep water and a clear toileting route available.
  2. Use gentle night lighting and traction on the familiar path.
  3. Check noise, temperature, and access to the usual rest place.
Which change preserves useful information?

Ask the right nighttime questions

You will bring a concise night pattern and know what needs faster help.

Night changes can have many causes. Share the sequence rather than diagnosing it at home.

  1. Ask whether pain, bathroom urgency, medication timing, senses, or cognitive changes need evaluation.
  2. Bring short clips only when they capture ordinary behavior safely.
  3. Ask which signs mean after-hours care rather than waiting.
What is the best appointment question?

Build a seven-night wake-up map

Show exactly when sleep changes and what finally helps your dog settle.

Nighttime pacing can have many causes. A simple sequence is more useful than writing only that it was a bad night.

  1. Record bedtime, first wake time, pacing, panting, drinking, toileting and settling time.
  2. Note any medicine, meal, noise, temperature or routine change that evening.
  3. Send the seven-night pattern and any safe short video with your questions for the veterinary team.
What belongs in a useful nighttime record?

Call sooner when you see this

Breathing difficulty, repeated unproductive retching, collapse, a swollen abdomen, inability to urinate, severe distress, or sudden confusion needs urgent veterinary advice.
